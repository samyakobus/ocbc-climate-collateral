## 2. How vendors and regulators enumerate hazards

The EU Taxonomy Delegated Regulation 2021/2139 Appendix A [30] is the reference list most vendors map to. It groups hazards into four families, each split chronic/acute: Temperature (chronic heat stress and variability; acute heat wave, cold wave, wildfire), Wind (chronic changing wind patterns; acute cyclone/typhoon, storm incl. dust storm, tornado), Water (chronic precipitation change, sea-level rise, water stress, saline intrusion; acute drought, heavy precipitation incl. hail, flood, glacial lake outburst), Solid mass (chronic coastal erosion, soil erosion; acute landslide, subsidence, avalanche). Vendors differ mainly in how many they turn into losses: MSCI (Apr 2026) scores 31 hazards but publishes return-period losses only for coastal/fluvial/pluvial flood and tropical cyclone; Munich Re Climate Change Edition gives 13 index scores at 2030/2040/2050/2100 under four SSPs [31]; S&P Climanomics runs 10 hazards through impact functions to average annual loss. Regulators in OCBC's markets name a narrower set: HKMA modelled "typhoons and floods" in its 2021 pilot [1]; BNM PD 028-124 lists acute "flood, storm, landslide" and chronic "sea level / temperature rise" and allows "higher discounts to asset valuations" [32]; NGFS (Sept 2025, co-chaired by BNM) reports central banks assess, in order, floods, droughts, wildfires, extreme heat, tropical storms, SLR, temperature rise, and lists free sources per hazard including NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 and Google Earth Engine [33]. BIS WP1274 (Jul 2025) formalises hazard-proportionate collateral haircuts [34].

## 3. What the evidence supports claiming to a board, and what it does not

Defensible claims: (a) typhoon wind and surge devalue HK/CN collateral, with a regulator-run stress test as precedent [1]; (b) extreme heat is capitalised into prices in China at roughly 0.1% per additional >35C day per year [6][7], and in the US appears in prices but not rents, i.e. buyers price long-run risk [8]; (c) chronic PM2.5 is capitalised into Chinese prices and rents [12][13]; (d) hazard datasets and 2030/2050 scenarios exist for all Tier 1 factors and are free.

Claims to avoid: no hedonic or default study quantifies heat, haze, landslide, subsidence, drought or humidity effects on property in Singapore, Malaysia or Indonesia. All heat elasticities are Chinese or American; the subsidence discount is Dutch [19]; the haze rent effect is Las Vegas [16]. The HKMA x25 ECL figure combines typhoon with flood and, in the 2051-2060 physical scenario, rising temperature with sea level; the heat-only share is not separable, and the headline numbers come from law-firm summaries rather than the HKMA press release. Humidity, rainfall-regime, lightning and dust have no property-price evidence at all. Insurance is currently soft (Marsh Q2 2026: global property rates -12%, Asia -5%) [35], so "premium inflation" is not a supportable claim today; "selective exclusions on cat-exposed assets" is. Several primary sources were paywalled; the HK 9.2%/C [24] and 24.8% UHI [11] figures should be re-verified before the pitch.

## 4. Concrete MVP proposal

Tier 1 factors joining flood in the haircut:

1. Typhoon wind (HK/CN only). Ingest STORM 100-yr wind [3] as a GEE asset; map to damage ratio via a two-occupancy curve (RC high-rise vs low-rise/industrial) and scale by loan tenor. Bands: <33 m/s 0%; 33-45 m/s 1-3%; >45 m/s 3-6%.
2. Storm surge. Swap the flood layer's coastal component to Aqueduct coastal wtsub [5] so surge and subsidence-adjusted SLR enter the existing flood damage curve, not a separate haircut.
3. Heat. Haircut = 0.1% x projected increase in >35C days/yr (NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 ssp585 2050 vs ERA5-Land baseline), elasticity from [6], capped at 5%, multiplied by a UHI factor of 1.0/1.25/1.5 by Yale SUHI tertile [10][11], reduced one step where Sentinel-2 NDVI within 300 m is in the top tertile. Label as borrowed elasticity; for SG substitute V3 2 km where the portal grant arrives [9].
4. Chronic PM2.5 (CN/HK). Bands on GHAP annual mean: <35 ug/m3 0%; 35-50 1%; >50 2%. Deliberately conservative relative to [12]; no scenario escalation because the 2030/2050 trajectory is downward.

Combination rule: take max-of within correlated acute water perils (fluvial, pluvial, coastal-surge), then add the wind haircut, then add the chronic sum (heat + air) capped at 5%, with a total cap of 25%. The cap sits well below the >50% devaluation HK banks judged possible in vulnerable areas [1], so the model errs conservative. Landslide (Tier 2) enters as a gate: parcels on >25 deg slope with LHASA exposure above threshold get a manual-review flag, never a numeric haircut, because the loss is binary. Show haze, water stress, cooling cost and subsidence as a context panel with the 2030/2050 direction of travel.

## Completeness critic

### Missing factors
- **Chronic sea-level rise / permanent inundation of reclaimed and low-lying land, separate from episodic surge**: Row 2 folds SLR into the surge damage curve, but permanent loss of usability (and of insurability) over a 25-30 yr tenor is a different mechanism from an event-based damage ratio. Reclaimed land in HK (Tseung Kwan O, Chek Lap Kok, Kai Tak), SG (Marina Bay, Jurong Island, East Coast) and Jakarta north coast is both low-lying and settling. NGFS and BNM list SLR as a chronic hazard in its own right.
  - Dataset: NASA/IPCC AR6 Sea Level Projections (regional, ssp245/ssp585, 2030-2150; not GEE, ingest as table); Aqueduct coastal wtsub for depth; Copernicus DEM 30 m (GEE) for elevation below +2 m; SG PUB Coastal Protection studies for local planning levels.
- **Regulatory adaptation status (engineered protection and planning levels) as a modifier**: The task brief lists this explicitly and the briefing omits it entirely. Singapore's minimum platform level (+4 m SHD, +5 m for new reclamation since 2011), the S$5bn Coastal and Flood Protection Fund, HK's Drainage Services stormwater tunnels and post-Mangkhut seawall upgrades, and Jakarta's NCICD giant sea wall all change realised loss for a given hazard depth. Without an adaptation term the model will overstate SG/HK coastal haircuts and understate Jakarta/Klang Valley ones, which a board-level reviewer will notice immediately.
  - Dataset: No global raster; jurisdiction-level lookup table (PUB/BCA Code of Practice on Surface Water Drainage, HK DSD Drainage Master Plans, BNM/DID flood-mitigation project list, Jakarta NCICD phases). Can be encoded as a protection-standard return period per admin polygon (e.g. FLOPROS global protection-standards database, Scussolini et al. 2016, sub-national, not GEE, free).
- **Saline intrusion / salinisation and chloride attack on reinforced concrete**: EU Taxonomy Appendix A lists saline intrusion as a chronic water hazard. In coastal Jakarta, Semarang, Pekalongan, the Mekong-adjacent markets and low-lying Peninsular MY, groundwater salinisation shortens the life of foundations and services and drives borrower capex; in HK and SG coastal high-rises chloride ingress is the dominant durability failure. Briefing has no row for it despite scoring surge and subsidence, which are its causal drivers.
  - Dataset: No direct global product; proxy from Aqueduct coastal flood depth x Herrera-Garcia subsidence x distance-to-coast (Copernicus DEM, GEE) plus GLDAS/ERA5-Land groundwater anomalies (GEE). Flag as mechanism-only, like humidity.
- **Coastal erosion and soil erosion as a separate chronic solid-mass hazard**: Row 14 dismisses coastal erosion as 'engineered out (SG SS 555)' but SS 555 is the Singapore lightning-protection standard and says nothing about coastlines; erosion is unengineered along Peninsular MY east coast (Kuala Terengganu, Pahang), Sabah, Bali, north Java and parts of HK outlying islands, and it is a chronic hazard in the EU list. It is also cheap to score.
  - Dataset: Deltares Shoreline Monitor / Luijendijk et al. 2018 global shoreline change rates (transects every 500 m, 1984-2016, free, not GEE but point data easily ingested); Sentinel-2 / Landsat shoreline extraction (GEE) for local updates; no CMIP6 scenario, use SLR as driver.
- **Wildfire / vegetation fire as a direct acute hazard (not only haze)**: Row 6 treats peat fire only via transboundary PM2.5 in SG/MY. For OCBC's Indonesian plantation-secured and Kalimantan/Riau/South Sumatra property collateral, fire is a direct total-loss and insurer-exclusion hazard (2015 and 2019 burned >2.6 Mha and ~1.6 Mha respectively), and CN/HK hill fires periodically threaten village-house collateral in the New Territories. EU Appendix A lists wildfire under acute temperature.
  - Dataset: FIRMS (GEE, daily); MODIS MCD64A1 burned area 500 m (GEE, 2000-present); Global Fire Weather Database (GFWED, NASA, not GEE) and NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 derived FWI for ssp245/ssp585 2030/2050; Global Peatland Map 2.0 (Greifswald/FAO) for peat overlay.
- **Insurance availability and retreat as a structured factor, not a sentence**: The task brief lists 'insurance retreat' as a required factor. The briefing only notes the current soft market. Board-material items: HK has no compulsory typhoon cover and the briefing's own 80% uninsured figure (unverified) means banks absorb the loss; MY flood cover is optional and increasingly excluded post-Dec 2021 Shah Alam floods; ID requires no property cover on most mortgages; SG HDB fire insurance excludes flood. The relevant metric is coverage ratio and exclusion frequency, which drives LGD directly and is independent of premium direction.
  - Dataset: No raster; country/segment lookup from OCBC's own loan tapes (insurance-in-force flag), plus GIA (SG), PIAM (MY), OJK (ID), HKFI claims statistics. Score as LGD multiplier by segment.
- **Cold wave / winter extremes and temperature variability for mainland China collateral**: EU Appendix A lists cold wave (acute) and temperature variability (chronic). OCBC/Bank of Ningbo-linked northern and central China exposures face pipe-burst, heating-cost and grid-failure risk (e.g. Jan 2008 southern China ice storm, Dec 2023 north China cold wave). Low weight, but a board reviewer checking against the Appendix A list will ask why it is absent rather than explicitly excluded.
  - Dataset: ERA5-Land daily minimum temperature (GEE); NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 tasmin (GEE, ssp245/ssp585). Score as 'explicitly excluded, negligible for SG/MY/ID/HK; context for CN'.
- **Compound heat-humidity (wet-bulb) and outdoor labour productivity for borrower income**: Row 12 treats humidity only as mould. Wet-bulb >31C days are the mechanism through which heat affects construction, plantation and logistics borrowers in MY/ID/SG (ILO 2019 projects ~2-4% working-hour loss in South-East Asia by 2030), i.e. PD channel not collateral value. The heat row uses dry-bulb >35C which understates SG/MY/ID stress where 33C at 80% RH is more dangerous than 38C in northern China.
  - Dataset: ERA5-Land 2 m temperature + dewpoint (GEE) to derive wet-bulb; NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 tas + hurs (GEE) for 2030/2050 scenarios.
- **Drought-driven hydropower and grid reliability (indirect physical risk to industrial collateral)**: Row 9 limits drought to corporate PD. The Sichuan 2022 heat-drought event forced industrial shutdowns for 11+ days and the Aug 2023 Yunnan restrictions hit aluminium; MY and ID hydro-dependent regions (Sarawak, Sumatra) share the mechanism. For industrial/logistics collateral, utility reliability is a valuation input (rent and vacancy), so it belongs in the context panel with a data source rather than being silently excluded.
  - Dataset: WRI Aqueduct 4.0 drought risk and interannual variability (sub-basin); TerraClimate PDSI 4 km (GEE); GRACE-FO groundwater anomaly (GEE); NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 pr for scenarios.
- **Tropical cyclone exposure in Malaysia (Sabah) and Indonesia (Nusa Tenggara), and tornado in eastern China**: Row 1 sets wind to 'zero' for SG/MY/ID. Cyclone Seroja (Apr 2021) caused >180 deaths and widespread building damage in East Nusa Tenggara; Typhoon Greg (1996) killed >200 in Sabah; Tropical Storm Vamei (2001) formed 1.5 deg N near Singapore. Low frequency, but 'negligible' should be 'low, scored not zeroed' for Sabah and eastern Indonesia. Tornado (Funing, Jiangsu 2016, 99 deaths) is on the EU list and affects Jiangsu/Anhui collateral.
  - Dataset: STORM v4 already covers the South Indian and West Pacific basins, so no new dataset is needed; simply do not hard-code zero outside HK/CN. IBTrACS (GEE) for historical tracks.

### Overstated claims
- 80% of HK households uninsured (HKFI 2019): Could not be verified in this session (search budget exhausted). HKFI's post-Mangkhut statements referred to low take-up of home contents insurance, but the 80% figure is not in the HKFI 2019 claims release cited as [2]; the cited Choy et al. 2020 paper is a loss-estimation study, not a household insurance survey. Attribute to a specific HKFI survey with a date or drop the number and say 'home insurance is not compulsory and take-up is low'. (source: Row 1 mechanism column; source [2])
- HKMA 2021 pilot: 1-yr mortgage ECL x25 (HK$0.7bn to HK$17.3bn) as evidence for typhoon wind: The briefing itself admits the figure combines typhoon with flood and (in the 2051-2060 leg) temperature and sea-level rise, and that the numbers come from law-firm summaries rather than the HKMA release. Using it as the 'Strong' evidence anchor for a wind-only haircut is therefore circular: the same figure is also used to justify the surge row. It should be cited once, as combined physical-risk precedent, and the wind row should rest on Mangkhut claims data alone. (source: Row 1 evidence column and Section 3 claim (a); source [1])
- Mangkhut ~3.4 m surge flooded Heng Fa Chuen / Tseung Kwan O: HKO recorded 3.40 m storm surge at Tai Po Kau and 2.35 m at Quarry Bay; Heng Fa Chuen is on the Quarry Bay side, so the 3.4 m figure does not describe the flooding at the named estates. Replace with 'surge of 2.35-3.40 m depending on location, maximum sea level 3.88 m at Quarry Bay'. (source: Row 2 evidence column; HKO via [2])
- GTSM 1-in-10-yr level +0.34 m by 2071-2100 RCP4.5 [4]: Muis et al. 2020 report this as a global-average change in the 1-in-10-year extreme sea level, not an HK or South China Sea value. Presenting it in the HK/CN row implies local applicability; local CoDEC points should be read directly. (source: Row 2 evidence column; source [4])
- Heat haircut = 0.1% x projected increase in >35C days/yr, capped at 5%: Kang et al. 2024 (verified: 'Feeling the heat: Extreme temperatures and housing prices in China', J. Economic Surveys) estimate a cross-city elasticity from historical variation of a few days per year; extrapolating linearly to SG's projected +20 to +330 days/yr and then capping is not a model, it is a cap. For SG/MY/ID the >35C dry-bulb threshold also misses the humid-heat mechanism. Label the SG/MY/ID heat haircut as a judgemental overlay, not an elasticity-based estimate. (source: Section 4 item 3; sources [6][9])
- Pang, Feng and Zhao 2026 'Sweating Assets' supports capitalisation of long-run heat risk into prices: Verified as a real J. Regional Science paper (26 cities, 1.5 m transactions). However, its abstract states the mechanism is reduced on-site property visits during warm seasons, i.e. a transient transaction-timing effect, not buyers pricing long-run climate risk. It supports the UHI/green-space modifier but not the 'buyers capitalise long-run risk' claim in Row 3. (source: Row 3 mechanism and evidence columns; source [7])
- Chronic PM2.5 evidence 'Strong (CN)': -0.54% price per +1% PM2.5 (Beijing) and +4.4% from clean-air policy (280 cities): A -0.54 elasticity is implausibly large relative to the international hedonic literature (typically -0.05 to -0.3) and, applied to Beijing's 2013-2022 PM2.5 decline (~60%), would imply a >30% price uplift attributable to air alone.
